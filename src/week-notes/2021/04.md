## Service manual

Up until the [register to vote](https://www.registertovote.service.gov.uk/register-to-vote/country-of-residence) service [went live](https://twitter.com/jaCattell/status/476247856899444736), the service manual was my favourite thing on [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/).

It continues to be an excellent place to understand how and why government uses [agile tools and techniques](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/agile-tools-techniques).

What's funny thou is the way waterfall and agile are merged in the [Discovery, Alpha, Beta, Live](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-alpha-phase-works) stages. Maybe it was done to appeal to "old school IT guys", as a former [Government Digital Service](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/government-digital-service) (GDS) leader once said.

The [Digital by Default Service Standard](https://gds.blog.gov.uk/2014/04/01/a-year-in-the-making-the-digital-by-default-service-standard/) figures heavily in the original service manual. These are the standards new services must meet at each stage, eg when being assessed before a move from beta to live.

Today it's called the [Service Standard](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard) and has changed in nature. The original had 26 things that teams had to demonstrate they'd done to achieve the standard. Nowadays it's just 14.

[Vicky Teinaki](https://twitter.com/vickytnz) has an excellent diagram that shows how the service standard has changed over time. It was published the same day I started typing this blog post.
