## Pension industry innovation

My 1ˢᵗ task is a new Digital, Data and Technology (DDaT) working group. TPR DDaT director [Paul Neville](https://www.linkedin.com/in/paul-neville-611a5a/)’s recent [blog post](https://blog.thepensionsregulator.gov.uk/2025/07/01/how-collaboration-will-help-pensions-thrive-in-the-digital-data-and-technology-age/) describes the group as:

- a cross-sector initiative to shape the future of pensions through DDaT transformation,
- a diverse mix of people including tech innovators, pension trustees, data specialists and voices from academia, civil society and other sectors,
- focused on key challenges like data quality, regulatory efficiency and digital integration.

Basically; bring us together and make things better (my professional motto).

## Open Data Camp

To prepare myself, I attended [Open Data Camp](https://www.odcamp.uk/) in Edinburgh this weekend. Was great to see [Pauline Roche](https://www.linkedin.com/in/paulineroche), [Mike Rose](https://www.mikerose.co.uk/) and others. Unfortunately the font of all knowledge [Owen Boswarva](https://www.owenboswarva.com/) wasn't there. I hoped to learn more about [Open Banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_banking) and how it might work in the pension industry.

So I pitched a session titled [Open pensions: Open banking part deux](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1MI_FhFCTEfFXFdgEttIW-nO8k8Rc9JtLGms_VQJHyL8/edit?usp=sharing) (that link takes you to the session notes) which 8 of us attended. I was very glad [Mary Drabble](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mary-drabble-0041842) was there to share her pension industry knowledge.  
  
Near the end of the session I asked for ideas to take back to TPR and got:

- **unique** (via [Dr Amy Roberts](https://bsky.app/profile/ar387.bsky.social)) - If needed, use properly unique identifiers that contain no personal identifiable information, e.g. random string of characters such as [0009-0003-2340-207X](https://orcid.org/0009-0003-2340-207X) which is the format used for [Open Researcher and Contributor ID](https://orcid.org/)s (ORCID),
- **opportunity** (via [Mary Drabble](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mary-drabble-0041842)) - Give pension scheme providers opportunities to help customers improve their financial strength and resilience (in return for their mandated help with the Pension Dashboard Programme),
- **clarity** (via [Naomi Penfold](https://github.com/npscience)) - Build for users, i.e. make sure whatever TPR or the pension industry design and build is simpler, clearer and faster (Naomi mentioned the [Pension Bee](https://www.pensionbee.com/) app has a good user experience, irrespective of their business model).

## Open pensions

A big focus of the TPR DDaT working group is pension industry data. Make data better, make data open. Surface it via APIs. This is helping the [Pensions Dashboards Programme](https://www.pensionsdashboardsprogramme.org.uk/) (PDP) which’ll let you see all of your pensions in 1 place.

I was at the [Service Design in Government conference](https://govservicedesign.net/programme-2025) earlier this month with Marion and others. There was a [brilliant PDP related talk](https://govservicedesign.net/programme/money-makes-world-go-round-how-service-designers-can-adopt-zoom-level-systems-thinking) from service designer [Alix Speed](https://www.linkedin.com/in/alix-speed-901a001a6/) and user experience (UX) consultant [Lia Emanuel](https://www.linkedin.com/in/lia-emanuel/). It was about how service designers can apply "zoom level" systems thinking to address public sector design challenges. The context was money guidance services, hence the PDP link.

Just before the talk started, Marion turned to me and playfully said, “This is your job!”. Turns out a large minority of pension scheme records aren’t digitised or available via APIs. The PDP needs this in order to give users what they need. So I’ll need to help the TPR DDaT working group tease these records out of the pension industry.

[Open Banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_banking) is a model that Open Pensions could use. But Open Banking took 8 years to implement. My contract with TPR is for 6 months. So I need to crack on and get the DDaT working group set-up and working fast.
